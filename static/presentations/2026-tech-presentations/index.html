Audience, Intent, Story, Delivery.

Communication is an important aspect of programming. So we should pay attention to it. We're going to explore these core concepts with a fun demo presentation at the end.

Audience

We need to know who are audience are. It tells us where we start from.

  • Background
  • Culture
  • Skill level
  • Reason for being here

Intent

Why are you giving this talk to this audience?

Every talk has the same general intent: to affect a (hopefully valuable) change in the audience.

Your talk will also have a specific, subject related, intent.

You need to know what this is.

A long time ago in a galaxy far,

(it's all about the story!)

If all you're doing is a dump of information, email it.

If you must give a presentation - tell a story.

  • It's interesting and engaging.
  • It gives you a structure for organising ideas.
  • It helps you think about movement of thought.

A rough heuristic for story telling is... these are not set-in-stone rules.

Beginning

All stories start somehow.

Beginning

  • Introduce yourself (make a connection)
  • Audience expectations (where are they now?)
  • Set the stage (context, key players, processes)

In a talk, use your beginning to make it intriguing.

Middle

The middle is where the drama happens.

Middle

  • There's a tension (found in the current context)
  • Describe the problem (unmask the source)
  • Why this is important (the value to be unlocked)

Find the inflection point, aha moment or reveal the devilish detail that everyone will agree is a pain.

End

The end resolves the tension and has a call to action (what happens next).

End

  • Explore (what could we do?)
  • Resolve (here's what I propose)
  • Reflect (next steps, and questions)

The climax to which we're heading, and it should leave the audience wanting more.

So, assuming you have a structure or framework to your talk, how exactly are you going to deliver it?

Delivery

Clearly you're going to have to speak, but you'll likely have slides to complement your words and share afterwards with the audience.

These are some *personal* tricks and tips to think about. Again, no hard-and-fast rules here.

The bullet point secret

  • Don't use bullet points
  • (Unless you're enumerating a list)
  • If you find yourself writing bullets...
  • ...put them in your presenter notes...
  • ...then choose an image to illustrate...
  • ...or a handful of words to make your point.
  • Never just read out bullet points. ;-)

Alternatives to bullet points

Surely there's a better way?

I have some ideas for reading out bullet points.

BOLD SUMMARY STATEMENTS

(With parenthetical clarification.)

You want the focus to be on what you're saying. The words on the screen give the core concept to keep people focused.

IMAGES

Again, forces the focus on the speaker, but gives people a break from words on slides. It helps the audience remain attentive and engaged.

Video's are a wonderful demonstration of how things unfold. Especially useful if a live demo is risky.

    """

In general, don't get lost in the details.

But if you need to illustrate something technical, a code walk-through is helpful and concrete.
